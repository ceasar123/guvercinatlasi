Adana Dewlap (Adana dalıcısı) pigeon

Breed Atlas

The dewlap diver of Çukurova (the plain around Adana). When it sees a pırıltı (a flapping pigeon) in front of its loft, it dives almost straight down to the ground; according to Özbek, it is bred not for its color but for its flight.

History and range

The Adana pigeon's name says plainly where it lives. Savaş and Erdem write that the breed comes from Adana and its surroundings, as its name suggests; however, they base this on two undated internet articles [1]. Özbek writes that the bird is bred mostly in Çukurova, especially in Adana, Ceyhan, Mersin, and Tarsus [4]; the team that made the Mersin measurements also lists the Adana, Ceyhan, and Mersin areas [3]. A 2012 compilation lists Adana, İçel, and their surroundings as the breeding region and also relays other names used in the area: Cins, Yerli, Töme, and Tartaç [2]. The researchers who measured birds in Mersin, for their part, note that the bird is also called Adana or Küpeli [3].

As for origin, the authors of the Mersin paper write that it is unknown [3]. Some sources claim that the breed descends from the dewlap pigeons of another region; Özbek notes that this claim is disputed, because the Adana pigeon is clearly distinct from those birds in both appearance and flight [4]. The same author relays that, in the hands of breeders in Türkiye, the birds have acquired a distinctive color, form, and style of flight since the Ottoman period [4]; the only basis for this part of the article is an internet site, and the statement is not a history supported by documents. According to Özbek, the breed is also bred in Syria today and is known there as Adana as well [5] [6].

The breed's recognition is not limited to Çukurova. Savaş and Erdem write that, in terms of flight performance, Adana is one of the favorite Turkish pigeons of Western breeders [1]. In Germany the bird is known as Adana wammen or earring dewlap [4].

The glint and the dive

Diver pigeons rise into the sky and go into a dive as soon as they are shown a glint. Pırıltı ("glint") is the name for the pigeon keeper's showing a white pigeon, held in the hand, to the bird in the sky [2]. The Adana pigeon is a well-known member of this group: when it sees a pigeon flapping its wings in front of its loft, it dives almost straight down to the ground at very high speed [1]. According to Savaş and Erdem, as the bird nears the ground, the sound of wind bouncing off its wings can be heard [1].

The introductory section of the paper from Mersin (based not on measurement but on relayed information) writes that these birds fly alone or in groups of three, that each group has a leader, and that the flight takes place at medium height. According to the same section, they come down when they see a bright object or a female pigeon, keep their wings closed during the dive, and open them to brake the descent and land [3]. According to Özbek, Adana pigeons are not flown for long periods or in large flocks; their diving style is especially different from that of other dewlap pigeons [4]. Group size appears in the sources with small differences: three [3], three or four [4], two or three [5].

As relayed by Özbek, the birds are kept in cabinets made up of small compartments with doors that can be closed, each holding only two birds; each pair has its own compartment, and the cabinet door stays closed at night [4].

How to recognize it

The body measurement of the Adana pigeon rests on 104 birds (52 male, 52 female) collected in 2018 from five separate places in the Erdemli, Mezitli, and Tarsus areas of Mersin [3]. The sources list the following features (the information on head, dewlap, and legs comes from the introductory section by Özbaşer and colleagues; it is relayed, not measured):

  • Head: It has a distinctive skull shape; the forehead bones form a straight line from the beak. These birds are considered large and big-headed [3].
  • Dewlap: It carries a fleshy pouch, called the gerdan (dewlap), running from the beak toward the neck [3].
  • Legs: The legs are curved [3].
  • Size: Medium size and a sturdy skeleton; the chicks grow fast early on [7].

The average live weight of the 104 birds measured is 505.94 g; 532.96 g in males and 478.92 g in females. According to the table, the average head length is 58.70 mm and the beak length is 20.87 mm. Head length and width, chest circumference, beak depth, and wing length differ statistically significantly between males and females [3]. The researchers report that the weight and some body measurements of these birds are higher than those of Şanlıurfa filo (squadron flyer) pigeons, the Ankara Tumbler, the Bursa Roller, and the Trakya roller [3]. These values are a sample from a single province and a single year; they should not be taken as the average of all Adana pigeons.

Colors and markings

Blue, white, black, red, and yellow are seen in the Adana pigeon, and each color has its own name: blue is called Şami [3]. Besides the colors, the markings on the plumage also have names [3]:

  • Aynalı: Large white patches on certain parts of the body.
  • Küpeli: White feathers starting behind the ear.
  • Zıhlı: Şami with small black spots.
  • Katrani: Şami with dense black spots.
  • Sakar: A white spot above the beak or in the middle of the forehead.

The source adds the names Muskalı and Safi to these [3]. The same paragraph also says that black is called Sakar as well; this name appears in the source as both a color and a marking. The Mersin sample included 42 blue, 27 red, 19 black, and 16 white birds; of the markings, Küpeli was seen in 18 birds and Katrani in 7 [3].

According to Özbek, Adana pigeons are not bred for their colors; color is not important in these birds, which are a performance bird [4].

Chicks and growth

Levent Özbek of Ankara University studied the growth of Adana pigeon chicks in a series of studies. First, the weights of 68 chicks over their first 43 days [4], and then the measurements of 88 chicks raised over seven years over the same period, were modeled [6]. According to Özbek's 2024 article, the pigeons sit on eggs two or three times a year, so an adult pair raises only one or two pairs of chicks a year; some of the chicks also die before reaching adulthood [6]. Weight gain speeds up in the first two weeks and then gradually slows [7]. Among the classical growth curves tried, the Richards model fit the data best [6].

Which group does it belong to? Classification and registration

The Adana pigeon is classified as a diver [1] [2]. In 2026, Savaş and Erdem compared 22 native Turkish breeds by a multivariate analysis based on behavior, body, and some hereditary traits. Although Adana looks close to the short-beaked breeds in the graph, the calculated distances showed it to be clearly distinct from this group; its nearest neighbors turned out to be Malatya and Muğla [8]. According to the authors, the apparent similarity to the short-beaked breeds clearly stems from the "diving" trait, and Adana is closer to the Malatya breed than to the short-beaked breeds [8]. In this analysis, the 2018 paper by Özbaşer and colleagues is cited as the basis for Adana [8].

In the study in which Savaş and Erdem examined male-female body differences in 2022, Adana Dewlap was placed in the same group as homing and high-flyer pigeons [9]. Most of the groups were formed according to the classification of the German show pigeon breeders' association; the homing and high-flyer group, however, was formed by the authors themselves, looking at breed traits [9].

Savaş and Erdem relay that two Adana varieties, long-beaked and short-beaked, are mentioned, and write that whether they can be counted as separate breeds needs to be investigated [1].

On the registration side, the situation is as follows: according to a 2022 statement by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, the registration of six pigeon breeds had been completed from 2004 up to that date [11]. Among the registered pigeon breeds is the Muğla Dalıcı Güvercini (Muğla Diver Pigeon), a name that includes "dalıcı" (diver); it was registered by a communiqué published in the Resmî Gazete (Official Gazette) in 2013. According to the registration document, the Muğla diver has no dewlap [10]; this is one of the differences between the two divers. No record is found of the Adana pigeon being registered as a breed by any official body.

Open file

What the sources state firmly about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

Known

  • The Adana pigeon is classified as a diver; when it sees a pırıltı (a flapping pigeon) in front of its loft, it dives almost straight down to the ground [1] [2].
  • Where it is bred is Çukurova; especially Adana, Ceyhan, Mersin, and Tarsus [4]. The team that made the Mersin measurements lists the Adana, Ceyhan, and Mersin areas [3].
  • Distinguishing features listed in the source: a fleshy dewlap running from the beak to the neck, a straight forehead from the beak, curved legs [3].
  • In the 104 birds measured in Mersin, the average live weight is 505.94 g (male 532.96 g, female 478.92 g) [3].
  • In the 2026 multivariate analysis, Adana's nearest neighbors are Malatya and Muğla; it stands apart from the short-beaked breeds [8].

Not yet studied

  • Origin: Savaş and Erdem base the claim that it comes from Adana and its surroundings on two undated internet articles [1]; the Mersin paper writes that the origin is unknown [3]. No document-based origin study is found.
  • Measurement: The body and color information rests on a single paper, the 104 birds in Mersin [3]; the 2026 comparison analysis also cites the same paper for Adana [8]. No published measurement made in another area is found.
  • Varieties: Two varieties, long-beaked and short-beaked, are mentioned; whether they can be counted as separate breeds needs to be investigated [1].
  • Standard and registration: No official registration record is found [10] [11]. No written source is found that defines standard details such as leg feathering, eye and beak color, and tail feather count, or evaluation criteria, or an association or federation standard.
  • Flight pattern: Group size varies in the sources between two and four [3] [4] [5]; the flight information in the Mersin paper rests on relayed information, not on measurement [3].
