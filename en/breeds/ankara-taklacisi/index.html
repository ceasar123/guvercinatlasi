History: a name, a guess

Most of what we know about the birds called "Ankara taklacısı" (Ankara Tumbler) comes from a study published in 2013. Atasoy and colleagues measured 199 pigeons on five farms in Ankara in 2011 and titled their study "Ankara ilinde yetiştirilen taklacı güvercinlerde (Columba livia domestica) morfolojik özelliklerin belirlenmesi" [Determination of morphological characteristics of tumbler pigeons raised in the province of Ankara] [1]. The title describes not a breed but a place and a style of flight.

Savaş and Erdem point this out in their 2021 review on the breed problem in Türkiye: "taklacı" (tumbler) is a very broad term, and many breeds can be counted under it. The authors guess that the birds measured in Ankara are the breed known as Takla, Mardin, or Oyun Kuşu ("game bird"); they write this as a guess, not as a firm determination [3]. In a 2022 comparison by the same authors, the Ankara (Atasoy et al. 2013) and Kırıkkale (Özçelik 2019) data are coded under the name "Turkish Clap Tumbler" [5].

Takla's names, its flight of diving toward the loft and "fişekleme" (roughly "rocketing"), what has been said about its origin, and the study on the brains of tumbling birds are collected on the Mardin Clap Tumbler (Takla) page.

How to recognize it

The most detailed description we have rests on 199 birds (108 male, 91 female) measured in 2011 on five farms in Ankara. The birds come from flocks in the hands of the public; the numbers per farm are 81, 35, 33, 29, and 21 [1]. According to this study, the features of the birds measured are as follows [1]:

  • Size: Average live weight is 328.49 g in males and 314.75 g in females. Average body length from beak tip to the longest tail feather is 34.95 cm, and average wingspan is 68.82 cm. Growth is complete by one year of age; the feathers keep lengthening with age. [1]
  • Head and leg feathering: In all birds measured, the head is oval and the legs are feathered (paça). Four paça types are described: normal paça, the length of the leg; sote paça, curled to the side and back; kılıç paça ("sword" paça), long toward the side; and bol paça ("full" paça), large and long toward the front (Kayseri paça). The paper does not give the proportions of these types. [1]
  • Crest: Nearly four in five are plain-headed (düz başlı); the others have a tuft (perçem), a cap (takke), or both. [1]
  • Color (don): About half are boz. Gök and dumanlı follow; black, white, and iridescent (yanardöner) are less common. [1]
  • Marking (nişane): Three in four have no marking. The markings seen are akkuyruk, kırkanat, and akbaş; according to the definition the paper relays from a pigeon-keeping web source, all three mean whiteness in the tail, the wing, or the head. [1]
  • Tail: Most (85.43%) have 12 tail feathers, fewer have 13 or 14. [1]
  • Eye: More than half have çakır (grayish blue) eyes; nar ("pomegranate") and gray eyes come second, üzüm ("grape") and light brown are few [1]. The Kırıkkale study renders çakır eye as grayish blue [2].

Relying on a pigeon-keeping source, the paper relays that a red eye is not wanted and the çakır eye is mostly preferred [1].

Color names change from province to province

Relaying from a pigeon-keeping web source, the Ankara study writes that the same color takes different names in different provinces: the color called Boz in Ankara is Sarı in Istanbul and Kırıkkale and Kızıl Sarı in Tokat; Ankara's Yanardöner is Çikolata in Kırıkkale, Nohudi in Istanbul, and Devetüyü in Çankırı [1]. Savaş and Erdem find the statement in the same text, "açık boza Şebap denir" [light boz is called Şebap], confusing. According to the review, although Turkish Sebap is thought to originate from the Takla breed, it now has features that allow it to be considered a breed in its own right [3].

The neighbors in Kırıkkale

Between March 2018 and January 2019, 80 tumblers (42 male, 38 female) from seven breeders in Kırıkkale were measured; the average weight is 323.28 g. According to the authors, the weight, body length, and wing measurements of the birds in the two provinces are very close. In Kırıkkale too, all the birds have leg feathering, but the classification is different, with three types counted: yıldız ("star"), silik ("indistinct"), and bol ("full"); yıldız paça is seen most [2].

Eye color, on the other hand, differs markedly: most Kırıkkale birds have brown eyes, and çakır eyes are very few [2]. Whether this difference comes from naming or from the birds themselves, the sources do not distinguish; the authors stress that color names change from region to region and are given by the breeders of that region. They interpret the prominence of the boz color in both provinces as breeders caring about plumage color as well as flight [2].

A separate breed? Name, distance, and registration

No definition of a separate breed under the name "Ankara taklacısı" (Ankara Tumbler) is found; the name describes the tumblers raised in Ankara [1] [3]. The authors of the Kırıkkale study suggest that the level of relatedness between the birds of the two provinces should be clarified by genetic studies [2].

In the 2026 study in which Savaş and Erdem compared 22 native breeds, the Ankara measurements and the brain study are cited as sources for Takla in the table; Takla clearly stands apart from all breeds [4]. This is not a DNA measurement; it rests on coding traits as present or absent [4]; details are on the Mardin Clap Tumbler (Takla) page.

On registration: the pigeons in the communiqué on the registration of native animal breeds are Bursa Oynarı, Trakya Makaracısı, Edremit Kelebek, Alabadem, Muğla Dalıcı, and Hünkâri; the names Takla, Mardin, or Ankara taklacısı do not appear [6]. The 2026 breed comparison also counts the same six breeds as registered [4]. Savaş and Erdem wrote in 2021 that although a pigeon federation exists, there has been no initiative toward creating breed standards (the same review also states that the federation created a standard for Şebap) [3].

Open file

What the sources state firmly about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

Known

  • In 2011, 199 pigeons (108 male, 91 female) were measured on five farms in Ankara; average weight is 328.49 g in males and 314.75 g in females. [1]
  • In all birds measured, the head is oval and the legs are feathered; about half are boz, and nearly four in five are plain-headed. [1]
  • Savaş and Erdem guess that the birds measured in Ankara are the Takla breed; they do not write this as a firm determination. [3]
  • In the 80 tumblers measured in Kırıkkale, weight, body length, and wing measurements are very close to those in Ankara; eye color differs markedly. [2]
  • In the 2026 study comparing 22 native breeds, Takla clearly stands apart from all breeds; this is not a DNA measurement. [4]

Not yet studied

  • When and how the birds came to Ankara: no written record is found; the origin cannot be documented.
  • A genetic study measuring the relatedness between the Ankara and Kırıkkale birds; the authors of the Kırıkkale study suggest that one be done. [2]
  • A written breed standard for Takla, and whether the name "Ankara taklacısı" (Ankara Tumbler) is used in the breeder world as a separate breed name.
  • Ankara measurements after 2011: the existing data rest on birds measured in 2011 on five farms, and more than two in five of them are from a single farm; the numbers reflect a sample, not a count of all tumblers in Ankara. [1]
  • Registration status: the consolidated text of the communiqué runs up to 25.10.2022; later amendments were not examined. [6]
