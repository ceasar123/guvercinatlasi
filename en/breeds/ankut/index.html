Ankut (Angut) pigeon

Breed Atlas

A solid-colored, medium-sized pigeon with an upright feathered crest running from ear to ear at the back of its head. Çelik (2023) describes this appearance under the name Angut; compilations that use the name Ankut, on the other hand, draw attention to its song. The sources do not say outright that the two names are the same bird.

History: from the courtyard bird to the first measurement

The most visible trace of the Angut in history is its place in Şanlıurfa's pigeon culture. A compilation published in 2012, grouping the pigeons raised in the city, counts the Angut among the "house pigeons" together with the Çiçi, Karagöz, and Kumru. According to the compilation, these birds are domestic; they are fed in a cage with its door open or in a high, open kuş takası (bird loft) of the house, and once accustomed to the house they never leave again [3]. Kuş takası is the name of the bird houses above the courtyard-facing room windows in traditional Şanlıurfa houses [3]. In the city's bird-keepers' coffeehouses, the birds are generally watched and their songs listened to [3].

Romedi Çelik's study, published in 2023, describes the angut as a breed specific to Şanlıurfa and writes that it has been bred for many years in Anatolia, especially in Şanlıurfa. The same article also relays that Şanlıurfa is cited as one of the centers where the pigeon was domesticated [1]. This is a view the article relays; it is not a firm archaeological determination. According to Çelik, the study is the first research to examine the morphological characteristics of the angut [1].

On the name, the sources disagree. Çelik writes "angut." In the compilations, on the other hand, the name appears as "Ankut," and in English as "Ankut Trumpeter." According to one of these compilations, the bird is also called Gut or Gud locally and is bred mainly in the Diyarbakır area [2]. The basis of this information is an internet site, and it does not match Çelik's Şanlıurfa finding. None of the academic sources says outright that the two spellings describe the same bird. A local news report covering a festival devoted solely to this bird in Şanlıurfa in 2020, however, uses both spellings together in the same text [7]. According to the report, the competition was held in red, yellow, white, and black colors, in male and female classes; no cash prizes were given to those who placed [7].

How to recognize it

The detailed measurement comes from Çelik's study of 146 birds (72 male, 74 female) raised in Şanlıurfa. The birds were divided into four age groups, from 6 months old to over 3 years [1]. According to breeders' statements and the researcher's observation, the angut is a form pigeon; the main criterion is that the color and body features are as desired [1]. According to this study, you can recognize the angut by the following features:

  • Crest: In all colors there is an upright feathered crest at the back of the head, starting where the nape ends and running from ear to ear. Called "kepez" in the paper, this rear crest completely covers the nape.
  • Forelock: The front crown, which appears in the paper under the name "ciga," is not fully round and does not grow long enough to cover the eye and beak.
  • Beak: Medium-sized, thin and elegant; average length 18.7 mm. Beak, claw, and eye color can vary with the bird's color.
  • Body: Medium-sized. The neck is thick and short; the chest is slightly curved forward, round and wide. The short body feathers form a flat surface and are not coarse or messy.
  • Wing and tail: The wings are carried over the tail and do not cross over the tail. The tail is not as long as the Demkeş's.
  • Feet: All anguts have long paça (leg feathering) curled backward. The feet are a little shorter than normal, and the stance is distinctive.

Source: Çelik 2023 [1].

Four solid colors

Four color varieties were identified in the study. Anguts have only solid colors; the variegated colors seen in the Demkeş and other trumpeter breeds are not found [1]:

  • Yellow angut: 41.1% (60 birds)
  • Red angut: 35.6% (52 birds)
  • White angut: 13.7% (20 birds)
  • Black angut: 9.6% (14 birds)

These proportions reflect a single sample; they are not a count of all anguts in Türkiye.

Measurements

The angut's average weight is 361.47 g; 358.6 g in females and 364.4 g in males. Unlike in many breeds, this difference did not turn out to be statistically significant. Weight rises with age: about 349 g in the youngest group and 371 g in the oldest. Average body length is about 34 cm, wingspan about 68 cm, wing length 29.3 cm, and tail length 13.3 cm. Males are significantly larger than females in wing length, wingspan, tail length, and head width. 89% of the birds have 12 tail feathers; birds with 13 and 14 are few [1].

For comparison: the angut is lighter than the filo (squadron flyer) pigeon (428.85 g) and the Çakal (374.02 g); and heavier than the Şanlıurfa Yapışanı (317.38 g), the Alabadem, the Ankara Tumbler (321.62 g), the Mülakat, and the Trakya Roller (335.58 g) [1]. Çelik interprets that, when the angut's measurements are compared with the standard measurements of its wild ancestor, the rock pigeon, the angut still retains traits resembling its ancestors [1].

Two names, two descriptions: a song pigeon or a form bird?

In Türkiye, pigeons are grouped by the purpose for which they are raised. The song of song pigeons is called "dem çekme" (drawing out a long note), and these birds are judged by the beauty of their dem çekme. In ornamental or form pigeons, on the other hand, performance is not looked at; the bird is raised solely for its outward appearance and beauty [2].

The compilations count the Ankut among the song pigeons. A 2014 compilation shows it, together with the Bayburt and Kumru, among the breeds that stand out for their song [5]. The classification in a 2024 article also lists the "Ankut trumpeter" in the same song group with the Demkeş and Kumru [4]. According to the 2012 compilation, the Ankut has a distinctive song called "dem çekme," and a good Ankut should be able to draw a dem for at least 40 to 45 minutes. The same compilation gives the same duration for the Demkeş as well; the basis of both pieces of information is internet sites [2].

Çelik, on the other hand, paints a different picture under the name Angut. According to Çelik, although anguts today resemble the Demkeş morphologically, they do not have the trumpeter voice. Trumpeter breeds take their names from their distinctive voice, which resembles a low laugh [1]. Çelik writes that the most distinctive feature separating the angut from the Bukhara Trumpeter is the front crown: in the Bukhara the front crown is round and long enough to make the eye and beak indistinct; in the angut it is not fully round and does not cover the eye. The rear crest is not as magnificent as in the Bukhara either. In general, the angut has a characteristic body shape that separates it from the trumpeters [1].

Çelik's conclusion is this: the data support the hypothesis that the angut is a form pigeon and should be defined as a separate breed [1]. Our showing the angut among the form and ornamental breeds on this page rests on that conclusion. Another classification also counts song pigeons under the heading of form pigeons [6]. The question of whether the voice was lost over time or the sources describe different birds stays in the Open file.

Registration

Çelik's study was carried out as part of the preliminary work required for registering the angut as a local genetic resource [1]. According to what the article relays, for registration an application file in which the breed is defined phenotypically and genetically is submitted to the Domestic Animal Registration Committee, and the committee's approval is published in the Resmî Gazete (Official Gazette). Çelik writes that genetic studies following the morphological measurements will support the breed definition [1]. No record of the angut being officially registered, or of a published genetic study, is found.

Open file

What the sources state firmly about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

Known

  • Çelik (2023) describes the angut as a breed specific to Şanlıurfa and takes measurements on 146 birds: average weight 361 g, tail feathers mostly 12, colors only yellow, red, white, and black [1].
  • According to the same study, the angut is a form pigeon; it carries an upright feathered crest from ear to ear at the back of the head and long leg feathering curled backward, and today has no trumpeter voice [1].
  • The compilations count the name Ankut among the song pigeons [4] [5]; according to the 2012 compilation, a good Ankut should draw a dem for at least 40 to 45 minutes, and the basis of this information is internet sites [2].
  • The 2012 compilation counts the Angut among Şanlıurfa's house pigeons [3]; in 2020 a festival devoted solely to this bird was held in Şanlıurfa, and the report uses both spellings together [7].

Not yet studied

  • Name match: No academic source equates Angut with Ankut as the same bird. The full text of the 2013 inventory by Yılmaz and colleagues, the most likely source that could clarify the match, could not be accessed.
  • Origin: No written source is found on where and when the breed arose.
  • Voice and regional difference: The reason for the difference between the descriptions, a silent form bird specific to Şanlıurfa [1] and a dem-drawing song pigeon bred mainly in the Diyarbakır area [2], is not clear: the voice may have been lost over time, or the sources may be describing different birds.
  • Measurement and standard: The detailed measurement rests on a single study and 146 birds; Çelik presents it as the first morphological examination of the breed [1]. Beyond this, no published measurement or federation or association standard is found.
  • Registration and genetics: Çelik writes that genetic studies following the morphological measurements will support the breed definition [1]; no official registration record or published genetic study is found.
