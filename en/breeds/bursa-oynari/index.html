Bursa Roller (Bursa Oynarı)

Breed Atlas

A flight pigeon from Bursa, typically black-bodied with white wings and a white tail. It flies scattered instead of joining a flock, stays in the air for hours and, according to Savaş and Erdem, was the first pigeon breed registered by the Ministry of Agriculture.

History: from Bursa to European lists

On the point that the Bursa Oynarı came from Bursa, the registration text and Savaş and Erdem say the same thing. Savaş and Erdem write that this is an origin "tartışmasız olarak kabul" [accepted as undisputed] [4]; the 2010 registration text also says the breed originates from Bursa and its surrounding area, is seen in Western Anatolia but is not widespread outside Bursa [1]. Today the bird is widespread in and around Bursa; it is also raised in Istanbul and Thrace [4].

More care is needed about the age of the breed. A scientific article mentions a history of 600 years reaching from the Ottoman era to today [2]. The report that Balcı and colleagues prepared for registration, while passing on this number, adds the note "bu konuda tarihsel dokümanlar incelenmemiştir" [historical documents on this subject have not been examined] [8]. So the 600-year figure is not based on a historical document in these two sources.

According to Savaş and Erdem, Germany's breed-poultry association (Bund Deutscher Rassegeflügel Züchter) adopted a standard for the Bursa Oynarı in May 1989; at Germany's application the breed was also registered by the European association EE [4]. The list of the German pigeon breed association still shows it today under the name "Bursa-Tümmler", gives its origin as Türkiye and describes it as "Kräftiger, flugfähiger Tümmler mit gestreckter Figur" [a strong, flight-capable tumbler of elongated build] [9].

In Türkiye, registration came twenty-one years later [4]. Communiqué No. 2010/27, published in the Official Gazette dated July 16, 2010, added the Bursa Oynarı to the list of native breeds as Annex 41 [1]. The registration rests on a report dated March 12, 2010 from a team at the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine of Uludağ University [2] [8]. According to Savaş and Erdem, this was the first pigeon breed registered by the Ministry of Agriculture; the authors attribute the delay probably to weak organization in Türkiye in general and to pigeon fanciers' inadequacy in organizing and systematizing their hobby [4]. According to a 2026 article, it is one of six pigeon breeds officially registered in Türkiye [7].

How to recognize it

The registration text describes the bird as follows: a medium-sized, long-beaked pigeon that flies for long periods [1]. Balcı and colleagues examined 43 birds (23 males, 20 females), 17-26 months old, randomly selected from seven lofts in Bursa [2]. According to these sources, you can recognize the Bursa Oynarı by the following features:

  • Plumage: Glossy black plumage and a black pied pattern; breeders much prefer birds with white wings and a white tail [2] [3]. The black neck feathers shine purplish, violet and dark green in strong light; this is called "şal" (shawl). In 53.49% of the measured birds the şal was purple [1] [2].
  • Head and beak: The head is mostly round (72.09%); less often, head types called kestane (chestnut), yılan (snake) and pelit (acorn) are seen [1] [2]. The beak joins the head broadly and is long; at an average of 2.57 cm in males and 2.62 cm in females, it was found to be longer than in other native breeds [1] [2].
  • Eye: Dusty rose ("gül kurusu") in 67.44% of the measured birds, white in 23.26% [2]. The registration text gives the eye color as "beyaz ve kirli beyaz" [white and dirty white] and describes a narrow, featherless ring around the eye [1].
  • Body and feet: The chest is broad; the wings are long, narrow and carried over the tail [1]. The legs below the ankle are completely featherless [8]; the feet are red, the nails white [1]. In the measured birds the nails were white in 95.35% and the underbelly white in 93.02% [2].

Types

The registration text defines the types by feather color and the distribution of white feathers [1]:

  • Karabaş: Black body, white wing tips and tail.
  • Yaşmaklı: White throat starting from where the lower beak ends.
  • Muskalı: White spot on the throat, under the beak.
  • Kalaça: White widening and then narrowing from under the eye toward the chest; the white also extends from the upper beak over the top of the head.
  • Beyaz and Siyah (White and Black): Solid colors. Seen rarely; mating the two can produce the typical white-winged, white-tailed bird. The eye is black in the whites.

Two measurements, two results

In the birds of Balcı and colleagues, average weight came out to 344.95 g in males and 338.41 g in females; males were significantly larger in body, leg and tail length, while there was no difference in weight between the two sexes [2]. According to the authors, the Bursa Oynarı is of smaller build than other native breeds [2].

In 2023, Şimşek and colleagues measured 100 birds aged 12-14 months, 20 from each of five types [3]. Average weight came out to 306.27 g and did not vary by sex or by type. The average of the measured traits was generally lower in the Yaşmaklı and higher in the Beyaz and Muskalı [3]. The two studies define body length differently: one measures from the first thoracic vertebra to the tip of the tailbone, the other from the longest tail feather to the tip of the upper beak; so the figures should not be compared directly [2] [3]. Şimşek and colleagues attribute the differences between the studies to type, age, care and feeding [3].

Roller or tumbler?

In the sources describing the Bursa Oynarı, flight stands out, but the sources do not describe it in the same words. The points that do not conflict are these: contrary to the flocking instinct typical of pigeons, the bird flies scattered, with hard wing beats, timid, as if fleeing from something [1]. Even when released as a group it disperses one bird at a time, so its flight is hard to follow with the naked eye [2]. It can stay in the air for 3-4 hours and finds its direction well [1] [2]; unlike many breeds, it does not respond to the calls known as "pırıltı" and "patırtı" [1]. It can suddenly rise and descend and change direction [3] [8].

The disagreement begins with the word "makara" (spool). According to one compilation, a roller pigeon does not tumble but glides downward, tracing a spiral in the air [5]. Another compilation describes this movement as "havada kendi çevrelerinde dönerek süzülmek" [gliding while turning around themselves in the air]; because it was likened to thread wound on a spool (makara), it was given the name "makara". The same compilation gives the measure of the glide as the height of an electric pole: a standard roller should glide at least one pole length, and good breeders aim for birds that glide 3-4 poles. Bursa is named first among the examples of roller breeds [6]. Şimşek and colleagues also describe the Oynar as one of the most important members of the rollers [3].

The registration text paints a different picture. According to it, the bird is "makaracı (birbiri peşine taklalar atan), taklacı ırk olarak bilinir" [known as a roller (one that does tumbles one after another), a tumbler breed]; above its nest it calms down somewhat and does 3-5 hard, hurried tumbles in a row, backward along the axis of the wings [1]. The report prepared for registration, however, writes that most of the birds do not tumble, that some birds tumble while beating their wings or with their wings open, and that 3-4 tumbles are also done above the loft [8]. In the German list the breed stands among the tumblers, under the name "Tümmler" [9].

Do these different accounts show different sides of the same bird, or does the word "makaracı" carry different meanings by region? The sources do not answer this question explicitly; no published study measuring flight has been found. A quantitative comparison comes from 2026: Savaş and Erdem compared 22 native breeds in terms of behavior, body size and some genetic traits. The distance between Bursa and Mülakat came out as 0.10; the authors interpret the two as birds of the same origin that differ only in color. The third breed closest to them is Alabadem [7]. Balcı and colleagues, for their part, write that the breed has distinctive characteristics that can be preserved [2].

Open file

What the sources state definitively about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

What is known

  • Communiqué No. 2010/27, published in the Official Gazette dated July 16, 2010, added the Bursa Oynarı to the list of native breeds as Annex 41 [1].
  • The registration text and Savaş and Erdem say the breed came from Bursa and its surrounding area [1] [4].
  • The registration text lists six types: Karabaş, Yaşmaklı, Muskalı, Kalaça, Beyaz and Siyah [1].
  • The bird flies scattered, one bird at a time, and can stay in the air for 3-4 hours [1] [2].
  • Average weight is 341.95 g in the measurement of 43 birds [2] and 306.27 g in the measurement of 100 birds [3]; the measurement definitions and the ages of the birds differ.

Not yet studied

  • Measuring flight: No published study measuring reel distance, number of tumbles and flight duration has been found; flight information rests on the registration text, the registration report and the compilations [1] [5] [6] [8].
  • Reel and tumble: The registration text, the registration report and the compilations describe the flight differently; whether the word "makaracı" carries other meanings by region is not answered in the sources [1] [5] [8].
  • Age and origin narrative: Historical documents for the 600-year history have not been examined [2] [8].
  • Genetics: Balcı and colleagues write that studies with larger groups, and especially DNA-based studies, are needed [2].
  • German standard: The 1989 standard and the European association EE registration are known through Savaş and Erdem's account [4]; the original text of the standard was not examined for this page.
