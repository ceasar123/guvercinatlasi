Scandaroon (Iskenderun pigeon)

Breed Atlas

A large, long-necked pigeon named after a Mediterranean port city. In Europe it is known as the Scandaroon; it is also raised in Hatay. Where it comes from, however, is disputed.

History: one port name, two continents

This pigeon is known in Europe as the Scandaroon in English, Nürnberger Bagdetten in German and Bagadais de Nuremberg in French [1]. Savaş and Erdem note that the names of some Turkish port cities appear in foreign pigeon names: the İzmir roller (Smyrna roller) and the İskenderun (Scandaroon) are examples. According to the authors, however, whether these breeds really came from these cities is disputed. That the İskenderun pigeon originates from this city is claimed because of its English name [1].

Old European pigeon books describe birds called Scanderoon and birds regarded as close to it. Darwin placed the bird called Scanderoon among the Runt breeds, which have long, massive beaks and very large bodies, and wrote that inextricable confusion reigns in the classification of this group. According to him, the Scanderoon of French authors was the bird that Boitard and Corbié called pigeon cygne and pigeon bagadais [5]. In 1824 these two authors had described the bagadais breed as timid birds recognized at first sight by their long, hooked beak, large body and extraordinarily long neck and legs [6]. According to Darwin's own description, the Scanderoon's tail was very short and carried up, its wings were very short, its neck long and distinctly curved, its beak long and slightly bent downward; its legs were long and its feet very large. According to Darwin, its main difference from the Bagadotten that comes from Germany was that the Scanderoon's beak was much less curved downward and that the skin around the eye and above the nostrils was almost entirely free of fleshy protrusions [5]. Another French book published in 1883 writes that the variety called the swan-necked bagadais has a neck that curves like a swan's, that its plumage is generally white, and that in the best birds the head forms a single curve from the nape to the tip of the beak [7].

The city itself, on the other hand, is associated with pigeons in another way. The same 1883 book, relying on the memoirs of the French consul Maillet, reports that in the 17th century pigeons were kept in İskenderun to announce the arrival of merchant ships to the interior. In the 18th century the arrival of ships was again announced this way, and the English trading house in Aleppo used this method in particular [7]. Boitard and Corbié, however, were more skeptical: they found very doubtful the claim that the people of Cairo, Aleppo and İskenderun carried news with a heavy-bodied "sahte haberci" [false messenger] pigeon; one of their reasons was that this heavy bird flew very badly [6]. The 1883 book does not state the breed of the birds in İskenderun; there is no document in these sources showing that today's İskenderun pigeon descends from them.

The history of the birds known by the name İskenderun in Türkiye today is not clear either. According to Savaş and Erdem, this pigeon was probably discovered in Europe and brought to the country in recent times; what is certain is that the birds known as İskenderun today were standardized in Germany [1]. A 2015 news report relays, in the words of the researcher who ran the project, that the İskenderun Chamber of Commerce and Industry had the birds of a research project in Hatay brought from Germany in pure form [8]. Savaş and Erdem write that most of the İskenderun birds probably brought from Europe are raised among "karışma" birds and that size in particular is sought in these birds [1]. Karışma is a method in which large flocks made up only of males are flown [1] [4]. The authors' conclusion is clear: what is known about their history in the region is not enough to call these birds "local" [1].

How to recognize it

The detailed measurements rest on 224 birds (122 males, 102 females) that Yıldırım, Doğan and Cımrın measured at six farms in Hatay in 2016 [2]. According to this study:

  • Size: Average weight 496.21 g, body length 40.31 cm, wingspan 71.40 cm, wing length 31.82 cm, tail length 13.78 cm.
  • Head and beak: Head length 66.33 mm, width 27.93 mm. Beak length 31.54 mm, depth 8.02 mm.
  • Eye: Red in 95.17% of the birds, orange in 4.83%.
  • Mustache (bıyık): A mark extending from under the eye toward the beak. The authors cite it as an important breeding trait in Scandaroons, but it was seen in only 13.53% of the birds.
  • Flight feathers and tail feathers (telek): 89.26% of the birds have 12 flight feathers in the wing. The number of tail feathers varies between 10 and 14; the most frequent number is 12 (53.78%).

Males averaged 516.84 g and females 457.59 g; wingspan was 72.64 cm in males and 70.15 cm in females. The effect of sex was significant in all measurements except beak length and shank diameter. Birds aged 6 to 12 months averaged 452.25 g, while birds aged 37 months and older weigh 520.24 g; the authors attribute this to the birds' continued development [2].

For comparison: the average weight of the Şebap is 355.49 g, and that of flight-flock (filo) pigeons is 428.85 g [9]. In wingspan, the İskenderun's 71.40 cm is greater than the Muradiye Dönek's 67.57 cm, the Ankara tumbler's 68.82 cm and the flight-flock (filo) pigeons' 67.67 cm [10]. Çelik, who measured the Şebap, notes that the Şebap's wingspan is close to that of a large İskenderun pigeon [9].

The description published in Europe

The same study also passes on a published European description of the İskenderun [2]:

  • Posture: Strong, very upright and proud.
  • Head and beak: The head is long, narrow and arched. The beak is very strong, both mandibles thick, the tip blunt; the forehead, skull and nape form an arc greater than a semicircle. The beak is whitish pink; in blue, blue-checker and solid-colored birds it can be light horn color.
  • Eye and cere: The eye is large; dark in whites and pied birds, in yellow tones in the other colors. The skin above the beak is bright red.
  • Body: The neck is long and sharply curved. The chest is broad and forward-projecting, the back broad and slightly arched.
  • Wing, tail, leg: The wing is of medium length, sits loosely on the body and rests against the tail; the primary flight feathers are short. The tail is short and closed. The legs are strong, featherless and red.

This description and the Hatay measurement do not match at every point (the comparison is this page's own observation; the article does not make it): while the European description gives eyes in yellow tones for colors other than white and pied birds, the eyes of the birds measured in Hatay came out predominantly red [2].

Colors

Five color varieties were distinguished in the Hatay study. 29.32% of the birds were red, 23.78% yellow, 20.13% black, 15.21% white and 11.56% blue-gray (gök) [2]. These proportions reflect a single sample; they are not a count of all İskenderun pigeons in Türkiye.

Pure or local? One measurement, two readings

The team that made the measurement ends its results with a warning. Under traditional breeder conditions, İskenderun pigeons differ morphologically from one another; according to the authors, the cause is random mating and breeders' lack of knowledge. The authors state that they determined, by subjective assessment, that the birds in the breeders' hands are not morphologically pure. They say the low proportion of the mustache mark could also be explained by the genetic makeup not being pure [2]. Their recommendations are to purify the birds' genetic makeup, to set up production stations at universities and research institutes for this, and to determine the breed's characteristics at the DNA level [2].

Savaş and Erdem, on the other hand, approach this study critically. According to them, concrete information on the claims in this area is lacking; the birds in the first and last photographs in the paper belong to the "Carrier" breed [1]. The researcher who ran the project, for his part, said in 2015 that morphologically very different birds are raised under the name İskenderun in the region and that there is no scientific work to fix a breed or type [8]. The project aimed to produce in pure form the birds whose genetics had changed through mixing with other breeds; it started with six birds and reached 25, and some were given to the İskenderun Chamber of Commerce [11]. According to the researcher, in an observation made with a camera placed in a room, contrary to the rumor that they cannot feed their young because of the structure of their beak, they were seen to care for their young with ease [8].

Classification also varies from source to source. One compilation lists the İskenderun in both its ornamental and its flight-flock (filo) pigeon lists [3]; another describes it as a "filo uçucusu ve süs tipi" [flight-flock flyer and ornamental type] [12]. In their 2022 comparison, Savaş and Erdem put the Hatay measurements in the form pigeons group. According to the same authors, flight-flock (filo) pigeons are not a breed but a group of relatively large birds that fly well and find their direction [4]. In the native breeds table of a 2025 compilation, "İskenderun Güvercini" is linked to Hatay [13].

On the genetic side, an international study examined 361 birds from 70 domestic breeds and two free-living populations and placed the Scandaroon, alongside the English Carrier and the Dragoon, in the group of breeds with fleshy beak protrusions. This group is large-bodied and among the domestic pigeons with the most developed beak skeleton; at the first split (K=2) it is among the main members of the same cluster together with the tumblers and owls [14]. The article's acknowledgments mention cooperation with American and German breeder associations; no sample from Türkiye appears in the main text.

As for registration: in the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's native breed registration communiqué, the Bursa Oynarı, Trakya Makaracısı, Edremit Kelebek, Alabadem, Muğla Dalıcı and Hünkâri pigeons appear; the İskenderun pigeon is not in this list [15]. The phrase "koruma altına alındı" [placed under protection] in the AA news report, by the report's own account, describes the production project at the university [11]; no official registration record has been found.

Open file

What the sources state definitively about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

What is known

  • In 224 birds measured at six farms in Hatay, average weight is 496 g, body length about 40 cm, and the eye is red in 95% [2].
  • In Europe it is known as the Scandaroon in English, Nürnberger Bagdetten in German and Bagadais de Nuremberg in French [1].
  • According to Savaş and Erdem, the birds known as İskenderun today were standardized in Germany and were probably brought to the country from Europe [1].
  • In an international DNA study the Scandaroon is in the group of breeds with fleshy beak protrusions, together with the Carrier and the Dragoon [14].
  • The İskenderun pigeon is not in the list in Article 4 of the native breed registration communiqué [15].

Not yet studied

  • Origin: Whether the bird originates from İskenderun is disputed and cannot be documented [1]; the İskenderun pigeons in the 1883 book have not been linked to today's breed [7].
  • DNA of the Hatay birds: No published DNA study on the birds in Hatay has been found; determining the breed's characteristics at the DNA level is also a recommendation of the team that made the measurement [2].
  • Measurement and sample: The detailed measurement rests on a single conference paper [2]; Savaş and Erdem state that the birds in the first and last photographs in the paper belong to the Carrier breed [1]. Which type the 224 measured birds represent remains unclear.
  • Standard: No federation or association standard for Türkiye has been found. The European description on this page is passed on at second hand [2]; the original text of the description and the European standard were not read.
  • Registration: No official registration record has been found; the phrase "koruma altına alındı" [placed under protection] in the news report describes the production project at the university [11].
