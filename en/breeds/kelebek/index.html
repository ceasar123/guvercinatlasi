Kelebek pigeon (Edremit Kelebek)

Breed Atlas

An Anatolian pigeon that spins around itself in flight, on an axis running from its beak to its tail. This page focuses on the Edremit Kelebek, the one among the breeds bearing the name Kelebek that was registered in the Official Gazette; other Kelebek types such as the Kemalpaşa, Kafa Kuyruk and Muradiye are covered in section 04, as far as the sources write about them.

History: the name's explanation is a guess, the trail goes back to 1940

The first part of the name is a place. According to the official description, the breed is raised mainly in the Edremit district of Balıkesir and its surrounding area, and also in some provinces of Western Anatolia [2]. The explanation of the second part rests on a guess. According to Savaş and Erdem of Çanakkale Onsekiz Mart University, it is "supposed" that Kelebek pigeons were given this name because, when flown in large numbers, their flight resembles butterflies [3]. So, a guess. The same sentence appears in a 2018 article and in the official description of the breed: the wing beats resemble the wing beats of a butterfly [1] [2]. Neither text ties the name to this resemblance.

On origin, according to Erdem, Konyalı and Savaş, there is not enough written information; a 2002 local book describing pigeon keeping in Edremit could trace the history of the breed back only to the year 1940 [1]. The authors of the article think the history of the breed probably goes back quite far [1]; this is an assessment, not a document.

The breed was registered on August 14, 2012. With the Ministry of Food, Agriculture and Livestock communiqué published in the Official Gazette that day, seven breeds were added to the list of registered native animal breeds: Edremit Kelebek Güvercini became Annex 49 and Alabadem became Annex 50 [2]. According to Annex 49, the purpose is hobby: the bird is raised for its flight style, its spinning movement and its colors [2].

The name Kelebek does not belong only to Edremit either [3]. According to Savaş and Erdem, the Kelebek is a Turkish performance breed that is quite common in Europe as well. According to the same authors, in Türkiye birds are selected not only for flight but also for their color and form, called "kostüm" (costume); in Europe only flight and spinning performance are considered [3].

How to recognize it

The measurements in this section rest on the study that Erdem and his team made in Edremit on 83 birds (36 females, 47 males) from ten breeders [1]. According to this study and the official description, the Edremit Kelebek is described with the following features:

  • Size: Medium-sized. Body length is about 36 cm in males and 35 cm in females. Wingspan is quite high relative to body size [1].
  • Chest and posture: The chest is not broad, slender compared with other breeds. The posture is relatively tall and slim. The wing tips are level with the tail tip [2].
  • Beak: Considered long; according to the article, about 23 mm on average [1].
  • Feathered feet (paça): All birds are feather-footed. According to the article, the feathering is wanted to cover the toes but not to grow too long; feathering that extends sideways is called "kılıç paça" (sword feathering) [1]. The official description, on the other hand, writes that the feathering reaches the tips of the toes and spreads out to the sides [2].
  • Eye: Usually black in white-feathered birds. The eye called "çakır" is very light, greenish white, with a pinkish surround. There are also birds with one black eye and the other çakır [1]. According to the official description, yellow and red eyes are not wanted [2].
  • Crest (tepe): 46% of the measured birds were crested. The crest is not very large, covering the nape; according to the authors, crested birds have been especially popular in recent years [1].

Color varieties

In most birds the body is white, and the color is either in the feathers in the middle of the tail or in the back of the neck, in the region extending from the nape to the head. In birds with a black neck, a white chest and throat are especially wanted [1]. The distribution of the 83 measured birds [1]:

  • Siyah tel kuyruk (black wire tail): 28%
  • Sarı boyun (yellow neck): 23%; the color is yellowish orange
  • Siyah boyun (black neck): 20%
  • Kırmızı boyun (red neck): 13%; the color is brownish red
  • Şeş: 7%; large black spots on white
  • Mor boyun (purple neck): 4% · Mor tel kuyruk (purple wire tail): 2% · Düz siyah (Arap) (solid black): around 2%

The color called "mor" (purple) in Edremit is called blue in many other breeds [1]; the official description also writes this color as Mavi Boyun (blue neck) and Mavi Tel Kuyruk (blue wire tail). Solid white or pied birds are also rarely seen [2]. These proportions reflect a single sample; they are not a count of all Edremit Kelebeks.

Flight and spinning

Edremit Kelebeks are known as "solo" flyers. When flown together, they fly in a jumble like a swarm of bees, in the article's comparison [1]. According to the official description, each bird released into the air chooses a direction for itself and usually finds its direction by the birds flying below it. Depending on training, a flight lasts between half an hour and two hours; the birds rise very quickly after being released [2]. The article's methods section states that Edremit pigeon fanciers were interviewed about flight characteristics [1].

According to the official description, the breed's main breeding purpose is the spinning movement in flight: the bird spins around itself on the axis between the beak and the tail [1] [2]. According to Savaş and Erdem, these birds spin mostly by diving or heading toward a pigeon they see below them [3]. The official description describes two spinning styles [2]:

  • Spin with the head (kafa ile dönüş): Flying parallel to the ground with the beak in front, the bird first closes the head and then one wing and makes a full turn with its whole body; it beats its wings once or twice in between and spins again.
  • Spin with the wing (kanat ile dönüş): The bird does not close its wings; throwing one wing over its head and pulling it back from the tail, it hangs in the air and spins to the side on which it threw the wing.

Among the sources of this page, these two styles are described only in the official description. No published study measuring the number, speed or height of the spins has been found.

Where do the numbers in the official description come from?

Annex 49 has a table of numbers: average weight of 336.9 g in males and 304.2 g in females; body length of 36.0 and 35.1 cm; beak of 23.6 and 23.5 mm; wingspan of 66.4 and 64.8 cm [2]. The description does not say where these numbers come from.

In their 2018 article, Erdem and his team gave the results in four groups: crested and crestless, female and male [1]. We combined these four groups according to the number of birds in each group. The resulting averages, together with the smallest and largest values, are the same as the table in Annex 49: 33 of the 36 values match exactly, and in the remaining three the difference is only 0.1. When a plain average is taken without accounting for the number of birds, the weights do not match. This calculation is ours; it is written neither in the article nor in the official description. So the measurements in the official description most likely come from the measurements of these 83 birds.

The official description also carries more than the article: it writes that the birds reach sexual maturity at 16 to 20 weeks, that their temperament is calm, and that their brooding is good [2].

The 2018 article writes that it would be important for the Edremit Kelebek to become a registered breed; when counting the pigeons that have been defined, it mentions the Alabadem, which was registered with the same communiqué, but does not mention the Edremit Kelebek [1] [2].

Its place among the other Kelebeks

In 2026, Savaş and Erdem compared 22 pigeon breeds of Turkish origin. For each trait, a single question was asked: is it present in this breed or not? In this comparison, the Kelebeks, Dolapçı and Dönek formed a group clearly separate from the others [4]. In the authors' table, the two breeds closest to the Edremit Kelebek are the Kemalpaşa (0.14) and Kafa Kuyruk (0.17) Kelebeks; they are followed by the Dönek (0.23) and Dolapçı (0.24). The breed it falls farthest from is the Takla (0.68), but the Takla is far from nearly all breeds in this table [4]. These distances come from marking traits as present or absent, not from DNA; so we draw no conclusion about genetic relatedness from the table.

According to the authors, these three Kelebeks probably began to diverge in recent times and are separated from one another almost only by color pattern; among the three compared, the crest is present only in the Edremit [4]. Breeders in Muradiye, which belongs to Susurluk in Balıkesir, want their Kelebek to be considered a separate breed because its flight style is different; the researchers write that the phylogenetic relationship of the Muradiye Kelebek with five breeds, including the Edremit Kelebek, needs to be determined by molecular studies [5] (details in section 04).

Note: Most of the numbers on this page for the Edremit Kelebek come from a single measurement study and from the official description that most likely rests on it. All the measured birds are from Edremit; 16 birds were measured from one breeder and 2 from another [1]. For this reason we do not generalize these numbers to all Kelebeks.

One name, several Kelebeks

In the sources, the name Kelebek does not stay only in Edremit. According to Savaş and Erdem, because the Kelebek breed is raised "locally" over a quite wide geography, differences in color and type have emerged mostly by city [3]. The same authors write that, even though there is not enough research, the Kelebeks have many varieties, some of which can probably be classified as breeds [3]. Erdem and colleagues also write that Kelebek pigeons are raised in various regions of Türkiye and show morphological differences according to the regions where they are raised [5]. In the five sources of this page, the following Kelebek names appear besides Edremit: Kemalpaşa, Kafa Kuyruk, Muradiye and, with only its name listed, Spotted Kelebek. Below, for each of them, only what is written in the source is given; details that are not in the sources are collected in section 05, the Open file.

Kemalpaşa Kelebek and Kafa Kuyruk Kelebek

Among the sources of this page, both types appear by name only in Savaş and Erdem's 2026 comparison of 22 breeds [4]. The article's breed table cites Savaş and Erdem's 2021 article as the source for both [4].

  • Region: The article writes that the Kelebek breeds are raised mostly in the same geography, in the Marmara and Aegean regions [4].
  • Registration: Their names do not appear in the Official Gazette's August 14, 2012 communiqué and the text of Annex 49; among the seven breeds added with that communiqué, the only breed bearing the name Kelebek is the Edremit Kelebek Güvercini [2]. The 2026 study, too, when listing the six registered breeds, writes only the Edremit as Kelebek [4].
  • Distinguishing feature: According to the authors, the Kafa Kuyruk, Kemalpaşa and Edremit Kelebeks probably began to diverge in recent times and are separated from one another almost only by color patterns; their flight characteristics are quite similar; among these three Kelebeks, the crest is present only in the Edremit [4]. All the Kelebek breeds are feather-footed [4]. The same article also writes that the Dönek, Kemalpaşa and Kafa Kuyruk are similar in terms of plumage color and pattern genes [4].
  • Measurement: For these two types, the relative distances in the article's trait table are given: 0.09 between Kemalpaşa and Kafa Kuyruk; distance to the Edremit is 0.14 for Kemalpaşa and 0.17 for Kafa Kuyruk; distance to the Dönek is 0.12 for Kemalpaşa and 0.11 for Kafa Kuyruk [4]. These distances come from marking traits as present or absent; not from DNA.

Muradiye Kelebek

For the Muradiye Kelebek, the basis is the 2024 article by Erdem and colleagues [5].

  • Region: The study was made in Muradiye, which belongs to the Susurluk district of Balıkesir; the place is about 23 km from Susurluk, in the south of the Marmara Region [5].
  • Registration: Muradiye does not appear in the Official Gazette texts. The article, too, when listing the registered native genotypes, writes only the Edremit as Kelebek; the Muradiye Kelebek is not in that list [5].
  • Distinguishing feature: According to the authors, the difference between the Muradiye Kelebek and the Edremit Kelebek is in flight style. The Muradiye Kelebek rises in groups in which many birds come together (in the article's words "shoal-shaped high fly", flying high in the form of a flock) and descends perpendicular to the ground. Because of these differences, Muradiye breeders insist that their birds be considered a breed separate from the other Kelebeks [5].
  • Measurement: 73 birds (35 males, 38 females), from six breeders [5]:
    • Head and crest: The head is large and round; 23.28% of the birds are crested (the article says "peak crest"), 76.72% crestless.
    • Eye: 69.90% grayish blue, 30.10% black.
    • Feathered feet: All the birds are feather-footed.
    • Basic colors: Ak (white) 11.00%, Arap (black) 8.20%, Samur (dark yellow) 6.80%.
    • Intermediate colors (eight): Kaplan şeş 17.80%; Çakal 16.40%; Kara kuyruk 13.70%; Bademli 6.85%; Sarı kuyruk 6.80%; Telli 5.50%; Mavi 4.20%; Karabaş 2.75%.
    • Tail feathers: Those with 14 are 24.70%; those with 15 are 39.70%; those with 17 are 19.20%; those with 18 are 16.40%.
    • Weight and length: In males 357.61 g and 37.83 cm, in females 320.06 g and 36.69 cm; overall average 338.06 g and 37.24 cm; overall average wingspan 64.76 cm (the article gives mean ± standard error).

The article also compares its own measurements with the crestless Edremit Kelebeks in the 2018 article: the weight and length of Muradiye males (357.61 g, 37.83 cm) are higher than those of Edremit males (339.25 g, 36.04 cm), and those of the females (320.06 g, 36.69 cm) are higher than those of Edremit females (310.50 g, 35.21 cm) [5]. These are measurements from two separate studies.

Mentioned by name only: Spotted Kelebek

Erdem and colleagues, writing that the pigeon breeds in Türkiye are divided into nine main groups, list the names "Kelebek" and "Spotted Kelebek" separately within the diving group [5].

The measurements and percentages in this section are from the 2024 article for the Muradiye; the Edremit numbers are not applied to the other types.

Open file

What the sources state definitively about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

What is known

  • The Edremit Kelebek Güvercini was registered in the Official Gazette dated August 14, 2012 (No. 28384, Communiqué 2012/61, Annex 49); the breeding purpose is hobby [2].
  • In Edremit, 83 birds from ten breeders were measured (36 females, 47 males); 46% of the birds were crested, and all the birds were feather-footed [1].
  • The breed's main breeding purpose is the spinning movement in flight: the bird spins around itself on the axis between the beak and the tail; the official description describes two spinning styles [2].
  • The name Kelebek covers more than one type: the Edremit, Kemalpaşa, Kafa Kuyruk and Muradiye Kelebeks are mentioned separately in the sources [4] [5].
  • In Muradiye, 73 birds from six breeders were measured; according to the authors, the difference between the Muradiye Kelebek and the Edremit Kelebek is in flight style [5].

Not yet studied

  • Origin: The origin of the breed cannot be documented. The 2002 local book describing pigeon keeping in Edremit could not be obtained; no record naming the breed from before 1940 was found.
  • Basis of the measurements: The 2018 article does not state in which year the measurements were made. The official description does not say how the numbers in the table and the information on maturity, temperament and brooding were compiled; that the table comes from the 2018 article is only our own calculation. The reason the article did not mention the Edremit Kelebek when listing registered breeds has also not been explained.
  • The mechanism and measurement of the spin: We could not find a study on the Edremit Kelebek examining which muscles, balance system or genes govern the spin; this does not mean that no such study exists. The number, speed and height of the spins also appear not to have been determined by a published measurement.
  • Kemalpaşa, Kafa Kuyruk and Spotted Kelebek: Region, registration status, body measurements, color varieties, eye color and spin description are not in the sources; for the Spotted Kelebek, no distinguishing feature is written either. For the Kemalpaşa and Kafa Kuyruk, all we have is the relative distances in the trait table. The breed descriptions of these two types are given in a supplementary document of the 2026 article and are not in the full text we have; in the full text we have of the 2021 study that the article cites as its source, neither name appears.
  • Relationship between the types: The phylogenetic relationship of the Muradiye Kelebek with five breeds, including the Edremit Kelebek, should be determined by molecular studies; in the sources we scanned, this question has not been answered.

If you have a federation or association standard, a measurement, an old photograph or a published study about this breed, write to us; we add to the page the information whose source we can verify.
