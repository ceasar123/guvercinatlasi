Turkish Misiri (Mısıri) pigeon

Breed Atlas

A small, delicate ornamental pigeon that has long been raised in and around Istanbul. It is recognized by its cube-shaped head, its large eyes, and, according to researchers, the shortest beak recorded in Türkiye to date.

History: one bird, many names

The Mısıri does not have a single name. Breeders also call it İstanbul Bango, İstanbul Mısırlısı, or just Bango. But Bango is not the name of a single breed; it is a general name given to short-beaked breeds in Türkiye, and it is used as the equivalent of the word "owl" in English [1]. According to Savaş and Erdem, especially in and around İzmir, breeds such as Baska, Polska, Azman, Bulgar Mısırisi, Mısırlı, and İstanbul Mısırisi are all called Bango [3]. In the Balıkesir region, on the other hand, almost all pigeons with a white body are called "Martı" ("gull"); Mısıris are also called Martı there [3].

The authors write that this confusion has led to crossbreeding among similar breeds, and that because some of these breeds are native breeds, their continuity is a cause for concern [3]. For example, the İstanbul Mısırisi, with colored wing covers and tail, is confused with the Bulgar Mısırisi, which has only colored wing covers [3]. A study that measured a hundred birds each under the names Bango, Mısıri, and Baska in Istanbul in 2020 found the Mısıri and the Bango very similar; the difference was the curly feathers on the Mısıri's chest [2]. According to Erdem and Savaş, that "Bango" had probably been mistaken for a separate breed because of misleading information from breeders; the birds were actually Bulgar Mısırisi and its crosses with the Mısıri [1].

There are guesses about where the name Mısıri or Mısırlı comes from; we do not guess [1].

From an archive document to Vienna

Erdem and Savaş state that they could not find an old written source in Turkish or Ottoman Turkish about the short-beaked breeds raised in Türkiye. According to the information the authors give, however, a document in the State Archives (dated 29.12.1341 in the record, converted by the authors to 12.09.1923) describes a type called "kravatlı güvercin" [cravat pigeon]: it has curly feathers on its chest, is a beautiful breed of small size, its head is square, its beak is short and small, and its eyes attract attention [1]. The name Mısıri does not appear in the passage the authors relay; the wording above follows the authors' English rendering.

In the older Western literature, there are notes that short-beaked pigeons were brought to Europe from the "East," or even from Anatolia. According to Erdem and Savaş's English rendering, Bungartz described short-beaked pigeons from Anatolia in 1890 in an ornithology journal published in Vienna: the skull was "ideal," the eyes were large, and the legs were red and unfeathered; because the tail was colored along with the wing covers, it would be more correct to call them "wing-tail." Erdem and Savaş find these birds very similar to the Mısıri in structure; where they differ is eye size and head shape [1].

Savaş and Erdem translate the Anatolian Owl in the European breed list as "Anadolu mısırisi" [Anatolian Mısıri]; according to the authors, although its origin is Türkiye, these birds were developed in a different direction in Germany [3]. These notes trace similar birds; they do not document the origin of the Mısıri.

From the sky to the ground

The Mısıri is originally a diving breed able to fly at medium heights [1]; birds in this group dive down when shown a rotating metal tool called a "glitter" or a female in the nest [2]. Today it is not flown, and two studies give two different reasons: according to breeder interviews, because of urbanization and tall buildings, the birds are now kept as ornamental birds [2]; according to Erdem and Savaş, on the other hand, they are not flown because of the difficulty of breeding them [1].

How to recognize it

The measurements below are based on 66 birds (37 males, 29 females) that Erdem and Savaş randomly selected at the 2021 Istanbul Mısıri and Baska Pigeon Exhibition [1]. In the authors' words, the Mısıri is "small and delicate"; compared with the other native breeds, its body remains small [1]. According to this study, the Mısıri can be recognized by the following features:

  • Head: Cube-shaped; because the beak is very short, this structure is even more pronounced.
  • Eye: Large; the eyeball protrudes slightly from the eye socket, which let the researchers measure it with calipers. Its diameter averages between 16.16 and 17.11 mm (right and left eye, female and male), with no significant difference between the sexes. Its color ranges from brown to dark brown.
  • Beak: Measured from the root of the nose to the tip, an average of 9.8 mm, that is, less than a centimeter. Its width, measured along the mouth opening, is greater than its length. Beak and nails are dark yellowish pink.
  • Chest: An ornament made of feathers that open and curl to the right and left along a line running down from the neck. No leg feathering on the feet, no crest on the head.
  • Tail: 12 feathers.

According to the Özbaşer Bulut team, this chest ornament is sparser in the Mısıri than in breeds such as the Turbit and the Oriental Frill [2]. Males are larger than females: body length 31.11 and 29.50 cm, wingspan 61.68 and 58.96 cm; the difference is also seen in body proportions [1].

The shortest beak: the authors' conclusion

According to values that Erdem and Savaş relay from another study (Savaş et al. 2023), among three native breeds counted as short-beaked, the average beak is 13.3 mm in the Denizli Azmanı, 15.2 in the Hünkâri, and 15.3 mm in the Manisa Azmanı; in the Mısıri it is 9.8 mm [1]. Erdem and Savaş's conclusion: the Mısıri is the shortest-beaked pigeon breed determined in Türkiye to date. It is clearly distinguished from similar short-beaked breeds by its eye and head structure; the Manisa Azmanı also has a chest ornament, but its feet are feathered [1]. In the Özbaşer Bulut team's measurement, however, the Mısıri's average beak came out at 8.08 mm [2]. The averages of the two studies do not match, and the papers do not explain why.

Pattern and color

In the wing-tail pattern, the feathers on the wing and the tail are the same color, and the rest of the body, flight feathers included, is white; in the tail pattern, only the tail is colored. There are also entirely white Mısıris. Colored spots in the white areas are considered a fault. No significant difference was found between the wing-tail and tail patterns in any measured trait; according to the authors, the patterns are not mated only among themselves [1].

Short beak and reproduction

Savaş, Erdem, and Tölü's 2026 study investigated possible hatching problems and chick survival in short-beaked pigeons. What follows is based on the study's published abstract. According to the abstract, extreme traits that deviate from the ancestral norm, such as the short beak in pigeons, can harm welfare; the abstract also notes that the beak performs essential functions such as taking feed and breaking the eggshell [4].

The researchers compared the breeding records of the short-beaked Mısıri, the medium-beaked Baska, and the normal-beaked Turkish Dönek. Hatching was 60.05% in the Mısıri, 74.34% in the Baska, and 84.62% in the Dönek; chick survival was 77.82%, 76.99%, and 89.39%, respectively. The effect of breed is significant in both traits. In a breeding season, the largest loss item in the Mısıri's incubation is death in the shell at 20.00%; chick deaths are seen most in the first ten days (26.79%). The authors find the Mısıri's results below what they should be compared with the Turkish Dönek [4].

According to Erdem and Savaş, Mısıris incubate reliably; but there are fertilization problems, and some of the chicks, probably because of their short beaks, cannot break the shell. The parents also struggle to raise chicks; for this reason breeders give the chicks to foster pigeons with medium or long beaks, or feed them by hand [1]. The abstract of the hatching study does not say who raised the chicks; for this reason, the chick survival rates should be read with caution.

The trouble is not new. Darwin, in 1859, relays the assertion that of the best short-beaked tumblers, more perish in the egg than are able to get out of it, and that fanciers assist in hatching [6]. Darwin's bird was not the Mısıri.

Breed or registered?

According to Erdem and Savaş, in local breeds that are easily distinguished by appearance and produced by a particular group of breeders, molecular genetic analysis is not a prerequisite for breed classification [1]. The Özbaşer Bulut team, on the other hand, recommends evaluating measurements together with genetic analysis and writes that the three short-beaked types urgently need to be protected [2]. In 2026, Savaş and Erdem compared 22 local breeds across 76 traits; in the multiple correspondence analysis, the short-beaked breeds formed a separate cluster: Akbaş Baska, Baska, Denizli Azmanı, Hünkâri, Manisa Azmanı, Mısıri, and Tokur [5].

The same authors count six officially registered native pigeon breeds: Bursa, Hünkâri, Edremit Kelebek, Alabadem, Trakya, and Muğla. The Mısıri is not on this list [5].

Open file

What the sources state firmly about this breed, and questions not yet studied.

Known

  • In the 66 birds (37 males, 29 females) that Erdem and Savaş measured at the 2021 exhibition, the Mısıri has an average beak of 9.8 mm; the authors consider this the shortest beak recorded in Türkiye [1].
  • Bango is not the name of a single breed; it is a general name given to short-beaked breeds in Türkiye, while the Mısıri is also called İstanbul Bango or İstanbul Mısırlısı [1].
  • The Mısıri is originally a diver able to fly at medium heights; today it is not flown [1].
  • According to the published abstract, hatching is 60.05% in the Mısıri, 74.34% in the Baska, and 84.62% in the Dönek [4].
  • The Mısıri is not among the six officially registered native breeds named by Savaş and Erdem (2026) [5].

Not yet studied

  • Reproduction and welfare: the data rest on a single study, and only the abstract of that study could be read; the abstract does not define what the loss rates are calculated against and does not say who raised the chicks [4]. No source turns up saying that Mısıri breeders assist hatching.
  • Name and origin: there is no written source supporting the origin of the name Mısıri or Mısırlı [1]. The State Archives document is known only from the authors' relay, and the date in the record is 29.12.1341, the authors' conversion is 12.09.1923, and this conversion was not independently verified; Bungartz's original 1890 German article was also not read [1].
  • Beak measurements: the averages of the two studies do not match (9.8 and 8.08 mm), and the papers do not explain why [1] [2]; the measurements of 66 birds rest on a single sample at a single exhibition [1].
  • Registration: no separate verification was made from the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete); the page rests only on Savaş and Erdem's list [5].
