Turkish Sebap (Şebap) pigeon

Breed File · Volume 1

An ornamental pigeon native to Şanlıurfa, with a large head and fluffy plumage. Today it is judged not by its movement in the sky but by its appearance on the ground.

History: from the tumbler family to the runway

The Şebap's story begins in Şanlıurfa. Researchers describe it as a breed native to this region and stress that keeping pigeons in the city is a tradition passed down from generation to generation. Pigeon enthusiasts are numerous in Şanlıurfa; the city has places where pigeon exhibitions and trading take place [1]. It is also often relayed in scientific papers that Şanlıurfa is named as one of the centers where the pigeon was domesticated [2] [4]. This is a view relayed by the papers; it is not a definitive archaeological finding. Pigeons in the city are also measured scientifically: in 2022 the yapışan tumblers of Şanlıurfa were studied [4], and a year later the Şebap [1].

In color and size, the Şebap closely resembles the Turkish tumbler. According to Çelik's 2023 study, the breed separated from the tumblers at some point and has kept its own characteristics for about a century [1]. Its relatives known as Takla are also called Mardin, oyun kuşu ("game bird"), or damcı in different regions; these birds dive toward the loft at an angle between 25 and 30 degrees, tumble, and then climb steeply. Breeders call this climb "fişekleme" (roughly "rocketing") [3].

The Şebap, however, has stayed out of this game in the air. According to the measurements, it is heavier than the game birds and has longer wings; its wingspan is close to that of the large Scandaroon (Iskenderun) pigeons. The researcher's interpretation is that this medium-sized body limits the agile maneuvers typical of tumblers and that the Şebap is therefore bred more as a "costume bird" [1].

For a long time, judging birds in competitions was the work of provincial associations. In 2013 these associations came together and founded the Şebap Pigeon Federation [3]. The federation is headquartered in Ankara [5] and 28 associations are under its roof [1]. The federation records include a list extending up to the 19th festival [5]. According to news reports, breeders from nearly forty provinces and nearly two hundred birds took part in the exhibition held in Erzurum Aziziye in August 2026 [6].

How to recognize it

The most detailed known measurement is based on 132 birds (66 males, 66 females) that Romedi Çelik collected in Şanlıurfa from an association and five breeders [1]. According to this study, you can recognize the Şebap by the following features:

  • Body: Medium-sized, with fluffy and soft plumage, a large head, and a thick neck. Beak length averages 17.8 mm.
  • Tail: Up to four white feathers in the middle, with the colored feathers on both sides arranged symmetrically. The tail cannot be entirely white. In the Arap variety the tail is entirely black. Breeders call the dark section near the tip of the light-colored feathers the "toka" ("buckle").
  • Paça (leg feathering): All Şebaps have soft, curled, overlapping feathers covering the foot. The paça is black in the Arap variety and white in the others.
  • Beak color: Bluish gray in Çakmaklı and Gök; vivid pink in Miski, Kürenk, and Arap.
  • Eye: In Miski and Çakmaklı, a pearl-colored eye turning straw yellow toward the outside; in Kürenk and Gök, a slightly orange, amber tone toward the outside; in Arap, a pearl-white iris.

The measurements also show differences by sex and age. The average weight of males is about 361 g, and that of females 350 g. Males are also statistically significantly larger in body length, wingspan, and head measurements. In the youngest age group the average weight is about 340 g, rising to 365 g in the oldest group [1]. For comparison: the Şebap's body length is greater than that of the Ankara tumbler and the Trakya makaracı; its weight is close to the Bursa roller and lighter than the filo pigeon. According to the researcher, this picture confirms the federation's definition of a "medium-sized breed" [1].

Five color varieties

The federation and associations define five basic varieties. Breeders call the pattern on the wing "pul" ("scale"). In the Arap variety the whole body is black. In the other four varieties the ground is a light, bright cream or bluish white, and the pattern falls on this ground [1]:

  • Miski: A dark, vivid brown, large and symmetrical scale on the wing.
  • Çakmaklı: A black scale.
  • Kürenk: A brown column pattern instead of a scale.
  • Gök: A black column pattern.
  • Arap: Black from head to tail.

Of the birds in the study, 57.5% were Miski, 16.7% Kürenk, 11.4% Çakmaklı, 11.4% Gök, and 3.0% Arap [1]. These proportions reflect a single sample; they are not a count of all Şebaps in Türkiye. The federation criteria add to these Abalı Miski and Abalı Çakmaklı, in which the scale covers the ground almost entirely; the most desirable is the one with "tam sıvama" (roughly "full coating") [5].

Scoring table and disqualification

According to the federation's written criteria, the Miski variety is judged under 11 headings out of 150 points: 25 points are given to general build, 20 to the scale, 20 to breast color, and 15 to the eye. In the Arap variety, which has no scale, the table has 7 headings and 110 points. In the Miski, the breast is required to be a reddish, vivid orange, the eye close to white, and the head as large as possible. The white feathers in the tail are called "ayna" ("mirror"); evenly distributed mirrors earn a full 10 points for the tail [5].

Some faults put a bird directly out of the competition: more than 16 tail feathers, whiteness other than the main color on the wing or head, a crest, tuft, or mustache, yellow or purple color on the nape, wing drop, missing or pulled feathers, and alterations made to mislead the judge. Sick, injured, blind, or lame birds are also not admitted to competition [5].

A breed? Measurement, DNA, and community decision

Çelik writes that the findings support the view that the Şebap is a separate breed, but that it would be meaningful for this to be supported by genetic studies as well [1]. A study published in 2025 took the first step in this direction: the team read the Şebap's mitochondrial DNA from start to finish with next-generation sequencing. The sequence is 17,232 base pairs long; it contains 37 genes, made up of 13 protein genes, 2 ribosomal RNA genes, and 22 transfer RNA genes, and the proportion of the letters A and T is 54.1%. On the phylogenetic tree, the Şebap fell within the lineage of the rock pigeon (Columba livia) [2]. This is an expected result, because all domestic pigeons are the domestic form of the rock pigeon.

Savaş and Erdem, on the other hand, look at the definition of breed from another angle. According to them, breeders calling a group with certain traits a breed is the easy side of the definition; genetic evidence is the hard side. The authors argue that molecular methods are useful for understanding kinship relations but are not strictly necessary to define a breed, and that looking only at DNA would leave out the cultural and historical side of a breed. The same review states that although the Şebap is thought to be of tumbler origin, it now has characteristics that allow it to be considered a breed in its own right [3].

In the federation's charter, securing the Şebap's registration is written as one of the aims [5]. In preparing this page, we could not find a record that the Şebap has been registered as a breed by an official body. It must also be noted that the detailed measurements today rest on a single study and 132 birds.
