Urfa Tumbler (Yapışan)

Breed Atlas

A feather-footed tumbler pigeon known in Şanlıurfa as "Yapışan." The city's bird fanciers keep it apart from the straight-flying "Halis" birds; a scientific measurement study of it was published in 2022.

History: from bird fanciers' coffeehouses to an article

Yapışan's story sits within the bird-keeping tradition of Şanlıurfa. According to the provincial directorate of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, bird keeping ranks first among the folk pursuits in the city; above the courtyard-facing windows of houses there are bird houses called "kuş takası" ["bird niche"] [4]. Those who keep and fly birds are locally called "kuşçu" (bird fancier), and the kuşçus meet in "kuşçu kahvehaneleri" ["bird fanciers' coffeehouses"] [3].

Urfa's bird fanciers divide their birds into groups. The provincial directorate's page counts "Halis kuşlar" ["Halis birds"] and "Yapışan (taklacı)" ["Yapışan (tumbler)"] birds separately [4]. According to a 2012 compilation, Halis birds do not tumble, they fly straight, and their legs are unfeathered; what sets Yapışan apart is the feathering on its feet called "tuman" and its tumbling (the compilation bases this on a website) [3]. The anthropologist Aslı Yazıcı Yakın conducted interviews in Urfa's bird fanciers' coffeehouses in the autumn of 2010 and noted that Halis and Yapışan are named as two separate kinds in the region. According to Yazıcı Yakın, what Ankara and many provinces call a "taklacı" (tumbler) is called Yapışan in Urfa [2].

Caution is needed on origin. The bird fanciers interviewed in Urfa say that the birds that went all over Türkiye are of Urfa origin; the researcher notes that the historical accuracy of this claim was not among the basic questions of the study [2]. Romedi Çelik's 2022 article also relays that Şanlıurfa is "mentioned" as one of the centers of domestication and that most of the breeds in Türkiye are "believed" to descend from Urfa pigeons [1]. These are opinions; they are not a firm finding.

Çelik introduces Yapışan as one of the important tumbler breeds that have attracted Urfa breeders' interest for centuries, and writes that until then there had been no study defining the breed's characteristics [1]; no second measurement published after this article has been found either.

How to recognize it

All of the Yapışan measurements in this section rest on 128 birds (65 males, 63 females) that Çelik collected from five local farms in Şanlıurfa. The birds were divided into three age groups, from 6 months to over two years old [1]. According to the findings of this study, the characteristics of Yapışan are as follows:

  • Size: Medium-sized; the head is large and the neck thick. Average weight 317.38 g, body length 33.29 cm, wingspan 66.59 cm, tail length 14.22 cm.
  • Leg feathering (paça): All Yapışans examined have leg feathering. The feathers are soft, slightly curled, and layered; they give the foot a thick look. The feathers above the elbow curve inward and outward and seem to cover the space between the legs, but they do not extend past the toes.
  • Beak: Bluish gray in the Gök variety, bright pink in the Beyaz variety. Gray spots at the beak tip were seen only in the Arap variety.
  • Flight feathers (telek): A 10-1-10 arrangement in the wing (counting the primary, axial, and secondary feathers in order) in 80.4% of birds. In the tail, 12 feathers 82.0%, 13 feathers 14.1%, 14 feathers 3.9%.
  • Eye: Çelik writes that the birds in the study confirmed older studies reporting a black "boğa gözü" ["bull's eye"] iris in white-feathered pigeons.

Three color varieties were distinguished by the breeders' assessment: 71.9% of the birds were Gök, 17.2% were Arap, and 10.9% were Beyaz [1]. These proportions reflect a single sample; they are not a count of all Yapışans. The varieties are shown with photographs in the article.

In all measurements other than tail length, the difference between males and females is statistically significant; males are generally larger. The females' average weight is 309.92 g and the males' 324.62 g. The weight, which averages 290.35 g in the youngest group, rises to 343.89 g in the group over two years old; the author reads this as the birds' development continuing [1].

For comparison: Yapışan's weight is close to the Classic Manisa Hünkâri (319.3 g) and the tumblers in Ankara (321.62 g); it is lighter than the filo (squadron flyer) pigeon (428.85 g) and the Trakya Roller (335.58 g). Çelik therefore regards it as a medium-sized breed and interprets that breeders focus on tumbling ability rather than weight [1]. The same author later measured an average of 355.49 g in the Şebap [7].

The article lists, as features that set Yapışan apart from other tumblers, a slightly angular head and a full forehead, and a ring around the eye that is less feathered and prominent; it bases these on a pigeon website. It also writes that the wings are carried below the tail, low down, but gives no source for this. These are not measurements [1].

Play in the air

Tumblers are pigeons selected for their ability to tumble or roll backward in flight [1]. A master in Urfa told Yazıcı Yakın that a tumbler matures in three stages: at one month old, while flying, it "kıç atar" ["tips back on its rump"]; then it begins to spin, and its spins speed up, which is called "yuvarlak" ["round"]; after that it stands upright, strikes, and climbs, which is called "havai" ["airy"]. According to the same master, a bird that comes and does a "çekim" ["a pull"] every five minutes is desirable; one that goes out with a very fast tumble spoils the work, and one that keeps on with 3 to 5 tumbles is considered desirable [2]. The researcher notes that masters describe these stages in similar words in Urfa and in Ankara [2].

In Urfa, some bird fanciers say that in a tumbler aesthetics matter even more than the tumbles: "Kuşun yüzü fincan gibi olacak." ["The bird's face should be like a teacup."] A bird fancier from Ankara, on the other hand, maintains that color and beauty are unimportant in a performance bird [2].

Opinions diverge on the reason for tumbling. Çelik writes, without citing a source, that this skill is believed to be a reflection of a survival skill that served to escape aerial attacks [1]. According to what Yazıcı Yakın relays, bird fanciers, for their part, are skeptical of such explanations; in their experience, a tumbling pigeon is caught very easily by birds of prey [2]. According to the same 2010 study, the limited scientific literature suggests that tumbling is hereditary, but its physiological mechanism had not been clarified at that time [2].

One name, two meanings: is Yapışan a breed?

The word "Yapışan" is used in two different senses. According to what Yazıcı Yakın relays, in Urfa the word is described as the bird losing its balance in the "yuvarlak" and clinging with its claws. The bird fancier from Ankara objects to this definition: in that bird fancier's view, yapışma is the bird, coming to play, landing on the ground without tumbling. In the researcher's summary, for one the word is the name of a kind, for the other the name of an undesired behavior [2]. In addition, in the tumbler competition rules the researcher examined, a straight landing without tumbling is also called "yapışma" and is taken into account in scoring [2].

According to Savaş and Erdem, "taklacı" is a very broad term and many breeds can be listed under it [5]; the names and the rocketing flight (fişekleme) of the breed whose international name is Takla are described on the Mardin Clap Tumbler (Takla) page. A 2025 compilation, in its table of native breeds, attaches "Urfa Taklacı" to Şanlıurfa as a separate row from the Mardin, Adana, and Sivas tumblers, but does not describe it separately [6]. No genetic or morphological study comparing Yapışan with Takla has been found.

Çelik's conclusion is as follows: the findings support the view that Yapışan is a breed, and it would be meaningful for this conclusion to be supported by DNA studies in the future. One of the study's aims was also preparation for registration; the author reports that work to register Yapışan as a local gene resource has begun [1].

  • All Yapışans examined have leg feathering; locally this feathering is called "tuman." [1] [3]

  • Of the measured birds, 71.9% were Gök, 17.2% were Arap, and 10.9% were Beyaz; 82.0% have 12 tail feathers. [1]
  • According to Çelik, the findings support the view that Yapışan is a breed; the author recommends that this be supported by DNA studies. [1]

Not yet studied

  • Origin: No written record showing when and where Yapışan arose was found; the Urfa-origin accounts remain at the level of opinion.
  • Measurement: The measurement-based information is gathered in a single article (128 birds, five farms); no second measurement was seen. This survey does not cover DergiPark's own search engine or a full search of TRDizin.
  • DNA and kinship: No genetic or morphological study comparing Yapışan with Takla was seen; the article also recommends support by DNA.
  • Registration: No official registration record was found; the article reports that registration work has begun. The names on the list of registered breeds were not read one by one.
  • Standard and description: There is no association or federation standard, no score table, no written feather description for the Gök and Arap varieties, and no eye color proportion. The competition rules are only general tumbler guidelines.
